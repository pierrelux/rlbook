# A cold pulse moving through a heated reactor

The [field-recovery control problem](../thermal_recovery/README.md) continues this example with direct-PDE MPPI. It uses a feasible 46% nominal operating field and begins control after the inlet pulse ends. The prescribed 48% heating examples below remain unchanged.


Open [the standalone replay](http://localhost:8003/interactive/thermal-reactor-mppi.html). **PDE dynamics** is the opening view. It plays once at 4× speed, unless reduced motion is requested. Pause or scrub to follow the cold region; choose **Change caused by the cold pulse** to separate its effect from the reactor's warm temperature gradient. The second heating schedule shows a prescribed response, not an optimized control.

The reactor contains 4,096 physical state values: temperature and remaining reactant fraction in each of 64 × 32 cells. The new scenario uses prescribed shear flow and a deterministic cold inlet pulse. The existing uniform-flow surrogate and MPPI experiments remain available in the other tabs; their checkpoint was not trained for this new scenario.

## What moves

Material flows from left to right in an 8 m × 2 m domain. Its downstream speed is

$$
v_x(y)=0.16+0.06\,4\eta(1-\eta)\;\mathrm{m/s},\qquad \eta=y/2,\qquad v_y=0.
$$

Flow is faster in the middle, so neighboring parts of the inlet pulse travel different distances. This prescribed, divergence-free flow has no transverse velocity; it stretches and tilts the patch without simulating turbulence or solving a momentum equation.

The baseline inlet temperature is $800+10\cos(\pi y/2)$ K. During 6–14 seconds, add

$$
\Delta T_{\mathrm{in}}(y,t)=-100\exp\!\left[-\frac{(y-0.55)^2}{2(0.22)^2}\right]
\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-6)}8\right)\;\mathrm K.
$$

The perturbation is zero outside that interval. Fresh inlet reactant fraction remains one. The colder material reacts more slowly, leaving less conversion as it travels. There is no random inlet forcing in this demonstration.

Both heating schedules start from the same state, generated by 400 seconds at 0.48 heating in all four zones under the shear flow. One holds that heating for 80 seconds. The other raises upstream-bottom heater H1 to 0.60 during 6–18 seconds. A separate no-pulse reference uses each identical heating schedule and initial state. The difference view subtracts its matching reference: blue means lower temperature or lower conversion. It is not a surrogate prediction error.

## Recorded behavior

| Time | Cold-region centroid | Maximum temperature deficit | Maximum reactant excess |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 s | 2.11 m | 39.80 K | 0.098 |
| 30 s | 4.19 m | 21.59 K | 0.129 |
| 40 s | 6.17 m | 16.65 K | 0.079 |

These values compare constant-heating runs with and without the inlet pulse. The centroid weights cells by their positive temperature deficit; it is not a tracked material particle.

| Heating schedule | Peak temperature | Minimum outlet conversion | Normalized heating energy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Constant | 1078.51 K | 96.09% | 38.40 s |
| Scheduled H1 pulse | 1097.47 K | 96.84% | 38.76 s |

The outlet conversion is weighted by material flux:

$$
\mathrm{conversion}_{\mathrm{out}}=1-\frac{\sum_j v_x(y_j)c(y_j,L)}{\sum_j v_x(y_j)}.
$$

Both schedules exceed 1070 K somewhere in the reactor throughout the 80-second recording. Neither falls below 95% outlet conversion. Violations are measured on the 0.25-second output grid, not certified continuously. These are physical response examples, not validated control policies. Heating energy is the time integral of mean heater fraction, not energy in joules.

## Numerical and display checks

The shared PDE retains conservative upwind advection, centered diffusion with zero boundary flux, temperature-dependent reaction, endothermic cooling, heat loss, and local heating. Nonautonomous SSPRK2 evaluates the inlet at both stage times and splits steps at heater switches. Substeps are at most 0.1 seconds and obey the maximum local transport/reaction stability bound. No physical field clipping is applied.

Across the 80-second pulse trajectory, halving the timestep changes temperature by 0.0033 K RMSE. Doubling the mesh to 128 × 64 changes temperature by 1.00 K RMSE and outlet conversion by 0.00169 mean absolute difference. The fine calculation starts from the same coarse cell averages using piecewise-constant prolongation. First-order upwind numerical diffusion is approximately $v\Delta x/2\simeq0.013$ m²/s here, larger than the physical diffusion coefficient of 0.0005 m²/s. Visible spreading therefore includes discretization effects.

The replay stores 321 actual PDE frames per run, at 0.25-second intervals. It shows recorded frames without blending candidate images or inventing intermediate fields. Temperature scales stay at 680–1120 K and conversion at 0–1. Difference scales stay at ±100 K and ±0.2 conversion. Scalar diagnostics always describe the absolute physical run, including when differences are displayed.

The [four-time storyboard](../../_static/thermal_reactor/plume-storyboard.pdf) shows the moving pulse under constant heating. Its rows show temperature, conversion, and temperature change relative to the no-pulse reference, with fixed scales across columns.

## Regenerate

From the repository root:

```sh
uv run python scripts/build_thermal_reactor.py plume
uv run pytest tests/test_thermal_plume.py tests/test_thermal_reactor.py tests/test_mppi_control.py
uv run python -m http.server 8003 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

The `plume` stage generates all four runs, checks refinement and exported arrays, and writes the storyboard. It never loads the surrogate, trains a model, or evaluates MPPI. Its binary recordings and dedicated manifest live in `interactive/thermal-plume-data/`; reports live beside this document. Opening the page only fetches precomputed artifacts. Original experiment artifacts remain in `artifacts/thermal_reactor/` and `interactive/thermal-reactor-data/`.
