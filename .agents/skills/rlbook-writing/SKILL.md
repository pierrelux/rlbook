# RL textbook writing

Read the repository's [WRITING_GUIDELINES.md](../../../WRITING_GUIDELINES.md)
before drafting or revising textbook prose. It is the maintained source of
house style, including detailed examples. For chapter structure or teaching
sequence changes, also read
[PEDAGOGICAL_GUIDELINES.md](../../../PEDAGOGICAL_GUIDELINES.md). Resolve these
paths relative to this skill directory; do not duplicate the source files.

Write for mathematically mature graduate students coming from ML and deep
learning who may be new to classical control. Preserve the author's intended
meaning, mathematical assumptions, level of detail, and requested language.

## Develop the explanation

Use complete paragraphs with strong topic sentences and explicit logical
connections. Explain why a definition, equation, or computational step is
needed, then explain what follows from it. Maintain consistent notation and
keep qualifications next to the claims they limit. Prefer enough explanation
to teach the idea over compressed reference-manual prose.

Apply the house style directly:

- State facts without announcements such as “the key insight,” “importantly,”
  or “it is worth noting.” Remove hype, dramatic transitions, and unnecessary
  adjectives or claims about the importance of a result.
- Use direct verbs. Avoid dressing ordinary consequences as things that
  “emerge,” “reveal themselves,” or “manifest.” Do not replace one stock phrase
  with another that performs the same announcement.
- Connect paragraphs with reasoning rather than inline bold labels. Use real
  section headings when the subject changes enough to warrant a division.
- Use lists for genuinely parallel items or ordered procedures; introduce
  them in prose and connect their contents to the surrounding explanation.
- Reserve bold for definitions, formal labels, method names in lists, and
  occasional necessary emphasis. Avoid bold sentence fragments functioning
  as slide titles.
- Replace em dashes used for pacing with commas, colons, or separate sentences.
  Avoid escalating “not only … but also” constructions, chatty asides, and
  rhetorical flourishes.

After revising, read the passage as continuous prose. Check that each paragraph
follows from the preceding one, each technical term is supported at first use,
and no change in wording has strengthened a claim or erased an assumption.
For explanations involving equations, preserve their mathematical content
unless the task calls for a correction; verify any correction separately.
