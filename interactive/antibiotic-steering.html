Steering a bacterial population away from resistance

Each node is one of the 16 combinations of four resistance mutations; the wild type is on the left and the quadruple mutant on the right. Under a drug, a population climbs to a fitter neighbouring genotype until no neighbour is fitter. Steering drugs are given first, then the final drug, and the goal is to avoid ending at its most resistant genotype (red ring). Compare a fixed drug sequence with the backward-induction policy, which looks at the genotype before choosing each drug.
