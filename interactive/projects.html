The project in one minute

  1. Pick a real system. Start from a paper in the control or domain literature that gives you a model or data, and numbers to compare against. No Atari, MuJoCo, Gym or toy robotics.

  2. Write it as a control problem. State, action, disturbances, objective, constraints: xt+1 = f(xt, ut, wt) and a cost.

  3. Implement a baseline. The published controller, a PID, a rule, or a myopic policy. Knowing how far a simple method gets is half the result.

  4. Let learning do real work. A learned model inside MPC, a value function, a policy amortized from a solver, offline RL from logs, a forecast trained for the decision.

  5. Show we can trust your numbers. Sanity checks on units and signs, limiting cases, a published result reproduced, sensitivity to parameters and seeds.

Problems that matter come first. We have a strong preference for climate mitigation, energy, industry, water, food and health. For the big picture, read Tackling Climate Change with Machine Learning (Rolnick et al., ACM Computing Surveys, 2022).

Poster checklist
  • One sentence states your main result; everything on the poster supports it.
  • Blocks for the problem, the model, the method, results with verification, and limitations.
  • One system diagram showing states, controls, disturbances and constraints.
  • Two to four plots, including your method against the baseline, each with a caption that states the conclusion.
  • 400 to 700 words in total. Above 800 you are writing a paper.
  • A QR code to your project website.
  • Print a draft on Letter paper. If it looks like a wall of text, cut.
Project website
  • One page, published from your repository (GitHub Pages is enough) and linked from a QR code on the poster.
  • At least one interactive figure that explains your core idea: let the visitor change a disturbance, a horizon or a cost weight and watch the closed loop respond.
  • Drive it with your real results. Precomputed traces from your runs are fine; a cartoon is not.
  • Show the baseline next to your method, and how you checked the numbers.
  • Keep it small: a few days of work. Content beats polish, and you must be able to explain every number it shows.
  • For the genre, see the demos in the book, such as the thermoacoustic pull-down in Iterative Trajectory Optimization or the aircraft replay in Model Predictive Path Integral Control.
